# ik-fiesta-bots review, 2026-11-24

A full review of the bot framework (C# host and library, the Lua leveller, the web UI and the
operational loop around them). It builds on `docs/AUDIT_2026-08-19.md` and says what changed since then.
The redesign that answers these findings is `docs/REDESIGN.md`.

**How this was produced.** Code read at `7cbf379` (branch `redesign-2026-11`). Live numbers were read from
the cluster host (`bots.ikaron.uk`, read-only GETs on `/api/bots`, `/api/gc`, `/api/bots/{id}/metrics`,
`/phases`, `/log`, `/script`) on 2026-11-24 between 21:09 and 21:11 UTC, with seven bots running
(`NewArcher`, `NewCleric`, `NewFighter`, `NewJoker`, `NewMage` on `level_quest`; `ArcherZero`, `PotionZero`
on `producer`). Friction points come from the kanban (`tickets.md`), the runbook, the project plan, the
agent memory notes, and the operator messages in the session transcripts that still exist (2026-10-15
onwards; the earlier bot-heavy sessions are only preserved through the kanban and memory).

**Rules for the correctness section.** Every game rule below is stated as observed or required game
behaviour, with an acceptance test that confirms it on the live server or a capture. Server-only data
tables (`MobInfoServer`, `MobWeapon`) are used offline as a reference, never at runtime (see section 6).

**Severity.** S1 = the bots stop making progress or die because of it. S2 = large, measurable loss of
efficiency or operator time. S3 = cost, risk or friction worth fixing during the redesign.

---

## 0. The headline findings

1. **Two of the five levellers have been standing in one loop for days.** `NewArcher` has been in phase
   `tent-clear-npc` since **2026-11-09 14:58 UTC (15.3 days)**; `NewMage` since **2026-11-21 18:16 UTC
   (3.1 days)**. Each re-issues the same `walkTo` to an unwalkable tile every ~0.55 s and logs
   `UNREACHABLE` forever. Nothing escalated it: the only CRIT lines in five hours of their logs are
   `SLOW TICKS` alarms. (S1, section 2.1.)
2. **All five levellers thrash between two phases** (`xpgrind` and `farm`) every ~0.6 s, for hours.
   (S1/S2, section 2.2.)
3. **Every action blocks the tick on the network.** `BotApi` has 62 `Wait(...)` calls plus one `.Result`
   that turn async sends, target confirmations, dismount waits, shop re-clicks and dialogue loops into
   synchronous calls on the Lua thread. A send can wait up to 5 s for the connection lock. Idle bots
   with no GC pressure still log 100 to 1,043 ms ticks. (S1, section 1.)
4. **The tick is 3 to 13 ms on average and 25 to 330 ms in the tail**, against a 1 ms goal. A full
   decision pass costs ~12.8 ms of Lua; average allocation is 240 to 415 KB per tick; a blocking gen2
   collection paused the whole process for 527 ms. (S1, section 1.)
5. **The leveller is a 10,475-line Lua file** with 307 functions, 707 top-level locals, 40 phases chosen
   by a priority cascade, 239 distinct `bot.*` calls at 2,170 call sites and 281 `crutch(...)` escape
   hatches. The C# side has four god classes of 2,000 to 3,900 lines each. (S2, section 3.)
6. **The operator cannot run the bots without Claude.** The web UI can relog one bot and un-deprioritise
   a quest. It cannot spawn, stop, start, apply or roll back a script, and there is no history of what
   ran when. (S2, section 4.)
7. **Combat geometry is guessed where the game's rule is exact.** Melee reach is a learned scalar capped
   at 150 u (the real reach against the smallest mob is 162.5 u and grows with target size); skill range
   ignores the target's body and the range tolerance; the chase leash is anchored at the mob's spawn
   instead of where it was last hit; the attack-block layer (`.shab`) is never loaded. (S1/S2, section 5.)
8. **The godot pathfinding fix is documented but NOT ported.** `NavMeshPath.Find` still routes with a
   centre-to-centre Dijkstra and still pushes region centres into the string-pull list, the exact cause
   of the 38 % "over 1.15x" paths measured in godot. The official client's goal snapping (which would have
   prevented finding 1) is not ported either. (S1, section 7.)

## 1. Performance

### 1.1 Where tick time goes today (measured live, 2026-11-24 21:09 UTC)

Tick metrics are recorded per tick and stored as 500 ms batch averages (`MetricStore.LogMetric`,
`Batch = 500 ms`), so the "p99" and "max" below are of batch averages and understate single-tick spikes.

| bot | ticks/s (5 m) | tick ms avg | p95 / p99 (batches) | max | alloc KB/tick avg | p95 | GC ms/tick avg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NewArcher | 17.3 | 2.8 | 8.9 / 25.7 | 51.8 (10 m: 127) | 241 | 261 | 0.06 |
| NewMage | 17.1 | 3.7 | 9.7 / 30.4 | 134 | 399 | 414 | 0.09 |
| NewFighter | 16.6 | 4.0 | 9.9 / 49.0 | 333 | 388 | 848 (max 1,954) | 0.11 |

- The loop runs at `tickMs = 50` (20 ticks/s cap); measured 15 to 17 ticks/s. The kanban's "70-100
  ticks/s" (2026-10-07) is no longer the operating point.
- The leveller's own section profiler (`[lqprof] TIME per sampled tick`) puts a **full decision pass at
  12.6 to 12.8 ms** (NewCleric 21:10, dominated by `handin-sell-skills` 4.3 to 4.5 ms and
  `scenario-state` 2.2 ms). The full pass runs every `PLAN_MS = 500` (`level_quest.lua:5456`); the
  ticks in between run `fastTick` (~1 to 4 ms).
- **Process GC** (`/api/gc`): 760,062 gen0 / 98,481 gen1 / 7,412 gen2 collections since start;
  11,010 s total pause; the last **blocking gen2 paused every bot for 527 ms**; NewCleric logged
  `[prof] TICK 474ms ... gc=287ms/1/0/0 alloc=2749KB` at 21:10:03. Pause share is 0.82 % now (it was 34 %
  before the Workstation-GC + 64 MB gen0 change in October), but the tail is still owned by full
  collections of a heap that the MoonSharp object graph keeps large (808 MB after the last ephemeral).
- **Allocation rate**: ~300 KB/tick x ~17 ticks/s x 7 bots ~= 35 to 45 MB/s, i.e. a 64 MB gen0 every
  1.5 to 2 s. Every collection stops every bot because all bots share one process and one heap.
- **Slow ticks with zero GC**: the two stuck bots, doing nothing but one failed `walkTo` per tick, log
  `SLOW TICKS ... worst 100-120ms (gc 0ms)` every 30 s and peaks of **1,004 and 1,043 ms with gc 0 ms**
  (NewMage 19:17 and 21:07). With no allocation pressure, that time is spent waiting (section 1.2).

Evidence: `GET /api/bots/{id}/metrics` (`tick`, `tickMs`, `tickAllocKb`, `tickGcMs`, `tickCrossings`),
`GET /api/gc`, `/log?level=note` for `[prof]` and `CRUTCH[CRIT] SLOW TICKS`.
Impact: S1. At ~4 ms average and 50 to 330 ms tails a fight decision is made at most 17 times a second
and sometimes not for a third of a second, which is a mob swing. The 1 ms budget is missed by 3 to 13x on
average and 25 to 300x in the tail.

### 1.2 Blocking network calls on the tick path

| site | what blocks the Lua thread | worst case |
|---|---|---|
| `BotApi.cs:779` `Wait<T>(Task<T>) => t.GetAwaiter().GetResult()`, used by **62** API methods (`cast`, `autoAttack`, `useItem`, `buy`, `sell`, `openShop`, `clickNpc`, `answerQuest`, `useGate`, `respawn`, trade, party, friend...) | every action awaits its sends synchronously | send-lock wait up to `SendLockWaitMs = 5_000` (`FiestaClientConnection.cs:21`) plus the flush |
| `BotApi.cs:1653` `travelTo` uses `.Result` | route resolution + travel start | unbounded |
| `BotManager.AutoAttackAsync` / `CastAsync` -> `AwaitTargetConfirmAsync` (`BotManager.cs:773-782`) | polls `Task.Delay(2)` until TARGETINFO arrives | 60 ms per cast or bash |
| `CastAsync` (`BotManager.cs:453-560`) | up to five sequential sends (target, mode, stop/face-step, cast) | 5 x send lock |
| `EnsureDismountedAsync` (`BotManager.cs:642`) | unconditional `Task.Delay(350)` then waits for RIDE_OFF | 8.35 s |
| `OpenShopAsync` (`BotManager.cs:2126`) | up to 8 re-clicks with 50 ms polls | seconds; the kanban records the producer tick blocked **23 s** in `openShop`/`doQuest` |
| `DriveQuestDialogueAsync` (`BotManager.cs:2452`) | up to 24 steps with 50-80 ms polls | seconds |
| `UseGateAsync` (`BotManager.cs:1239`) | 150 ms waits around the gate menu | seconds |

- Sync-over-async on 7 dedicated Lua threads also needs thread-pool threads to run the continuations;
  when those are busy (the read loops, NavPlanner workers, HTTP) the wait stretches. This is the most
  likely source of the 1 s zero-GC ticks; the instrument to prove it does not exist (no per-call wall
  time outside the sampled profile).
- A broken socket surfaces as a Lua error: `NewFighter` `lastError = "Unable to write data to the
  transport connection: Broken pipe."` (`GET /api/bots/NewFighter/script`). Transport failures reach the
  decision code.

Impact: S1. Network latency and lock contention become decision latency; a slow shop blocks combat.

### 1.3 The Lua/C# boundary

- MoonSharp marshals every `bot.*` call through reflection-based userdata dispatch; the profiler measured
  **~10 to 12 KB allocated per crossing** and `nearbyMobs` at **100 to 190 KB per call**
  (`BotScriptRunner.cs` ProfileShim notes; memory note on GC pauses). Tables returned to Lua are rebuilt
  per call.
- The fix so far is a memo shim in Lua (`BotScriptRunner.cs` `ProfileShim`, `memoK`/`tickK` lists) that
  caches 30+ calls per tick. It works (live crossings are now 1.5 to 2.9 per tick on average, p99 up to
  41 on NewFighter) but it is a correctness hazard: whether a value may be cached is decided by a
  hand-kept list in a C# string literal, and the comments in it contradict each other twice
  (`skillInfo` and `invenCountOf` are each described as both enrolled and excluded).
- The leveller adds its own caches on top (`pulse`, `freshPulse`, `activeKill` memo, `routeCache`); the
  2026-08-20 nine-hour wedge was a stale Lua cache (`AUDIT_2026-08-19.md` postscript).
- Event dispatch builds Lua tables on the tick thread (`BotScriptRunner.DispatchCore`); it was the hidden
  ~450 ms per cycle in October and is now guarded by `Handles(fn)`.

Impact: S2. The boundary is the main allocator and forces caching discipline into the wrong layer.

### 1.4 Threading and state ownership

- `ZoneView` (3,868 lines) is mutated by the session read loop and read concurrently by the Lua thread,
  the HTTP threads, the NavPlanner worker and the watchdog. It holds **45 `ConcurrentDictionary`
  fields** and **81 `DateTime.UtcNow` reads**. No tick sees a consistent snapshot: HP, position,
  aggressors and the mob list can each change between two reads in one decision.
- Per bot there is a Lua thread, a read loop, a NavPlanner drain task, a watchdog task, optional follow /
  autoloot tasks and the event hub. There is no single owner of a bot's state, so every fix adds a lock,
  a `Volatile.Read`, or a "freshness" timestamp.
- The pathfinding grid is shared per map (`BotEndpoints.LoadGrid`), including the learned MOVEFAIL blocks
  (`BlockGrid.RtBlocked`) and door overrides. One bot's wrong lesson re-routes every bot on that map, and
  `RouteAndWalk` clears it for everyone when it severs a route (`BotApi.cs` `RouteAndWalk`,
  `ClearRuntimeBlocked`).
- `DateTime.UtcNow` everywhere makes the decision code non-deterministic and impossible to replay.

Impact: S2. This is the root of the "stale belief" bug family (shapes B, C, E in the August audit).

### 1.5 Measurement gaps

- The per-call profile is sampled 1 tick in 10 and resets per window; `GET /api/bots/{id}/profile`
  returned `calls: 0, rows: []` for all three bots at 21:09 (the window had just been sampled clean), so
  the endpoint answers "where did time go" only by luck.
- There is no per-action wall-time metric, so blocking waits (section 1.2) are invisible except as
  unexplained slow ticks.
- No benchmark runs in CI; the 1 ms goal has no test.

## 2. Decision quality ("stuck", kiting, target choice)

### 2.1 The 15-day tent loop (S1, live now)

Evidence (`/api/bots/NewArcher/phases`, `/log?level=info&max=14`, 21:10 UTC):

```
open visit: tent-clear-npc  started 14:58:57.997  seconds 1318273.7   (2026-11-09)
21:10:35.847 [tent] NPC h=17282 72u away (need > 1035u) - walking clear before pitching
21:10:35.853 [nav] walkTo (12007,11509) UNREACHABLE on Eld after 3ms - start=(1888,1674)walk=True goal=(1921,1841)walk=False
... repeated every ~0.55 s
```

`NewMage` is identical on another NPC since 2026-11-21 18:16 (`need > 2853u`, goal tile `walk=False`).

- The tent logic (`level_quest.lua` `tentTick`, ~2958-3020) learns an NPC clearance from refusals, then
  walks directly away from the nearest NPC by that distance. The computed point is inside a building.
  `walkTo` fails; the next tick recomputes the same point; nothing counts the failures, nothing changes
  the plan, nothing leaves the phase.
- The learned clearance only grows (`math.max`), so one refusal next to a large building can make the
  requirement unsatisfiable in a town.
- The real client never has this problem: when a click lands on a blocked tile it snaps the destination
  to the nearest walkable tile (ring search toward the start; ported in fiesta-godot as
  `FiestaWalkGrid::search_moveable`). The bot has no equivalent (section 7).
- The wedge detectors did not fire: the bot is in town, not losing HP, and its "movement" attempts count
  as activity. The phase-visit endpoint prints `HH:mm:ss` with no date (`BotEndpoints.cs:289`), so even
  the evidence reads like "since this afternoon".

Root cause class: **a state with no failure exit and no progress measure.** This is the most common
stuck shape in the kanban (q32 shop loop, Iyzel gate park, revive-ignored loop, the travel freeze after a
mid-transit fight, "wedged off-grid"), and it is structural: the leveller's phases are not states with
declared exits, so "this isn't working" has nowhere to go except a new crutch.

### 2.2 Phase thrash: `xpgrind` <-> `farm` every 0.6 s (S1/S2, live now)

All five levellers' last 5,000 phase visits alternate `xpgrind` (0.6 s) and `farm` (0 to 0.1 s), with
`storage:travel-to-keeper` interleaved. Totals over each bot's window (`/phases`):

| bot | window | biggest buckets |
|---|---|---|
| NewCleric | ~10 h | `storage:travel-to-keeper`, `farm`, `xpgrind`; `kill (quest mobs)` 220 s |
| NewFighter | ~2.3 h | `storage:travel-to-keeper` 3,888 s, `farm` 2,254 s, `xpgrind` 1,546 s; `kill (quest mobs)` 101 s |
| NewJoker | ~2.7 h | `storage:travel-to-keeper` 4,695 s, `farm` 1,701 s, `xpgrind` 1,532 s; `kill (quest mobs)` 98 s |

- Quest-mob kills are ~2 to 3 % of the time. Bags are full (`NewArcher bagFree 0/48`, 0 HP and SP
  stones, 40 % HP), so most of the clock goes to storage trips.
- Two phases that each think the other should run, re-decided every full pass (`PLAN_MS = 500`) with no
  hysteresis, minimum dwell or commitment, produce exactly this. The kanban's "travel ValDn01 was 31 % of
  NewFighter's time" and "phase=restock through three MOTIONLESS watchdogs" are the same shape.

### 2.3 Kiting and fights (from the kanban, measured by the bots)

- **Kiting cancels our own damage.** NewMage 2026-10-08: 7 casts / 4 hits in 2.5 min of combat, SP flat
  at 509; "a cast needs us standing still, so continuous kiting = no damage". Same for melee: any move
  stops auto-attack (section 5.5). The kite loop has no notion of "gain gap, stop, cast, repeat".
- **Pack pulls on dense fields**: NewMage 7 deaths and NewArcher 6 deaths in 11 minutes (2026-10-08) on
  level-appropriate fields; "hook" walks to 41 u inside the pack; drags call "isolated" after 1 s without
  moving; the bot re-engages the same pack six times in two minutes. The model lacks the family-assist
  radius (section 5.8) and remembers no "this spot outmatched me".
- **Target selection** walked past a 200 u mob for one 1,500 u away (fixed 2026-09-12: lexicographic
  ranking); the corridor risk is now priced, but there is still no single scoring surface that the
  operator can see per candidate.
- **Chase/shed decisions use the wrong leash** (section 5.6), so "how far until it gives up" is wrong
  whenever the bot hits a mob while retreating.
- **Mid-fight self-heals refused 0x0FC0** (NewCleric, 18 refusals, 2026-10-08), still open; and three
  refusal paths in `CastAsync` return `ActionResult.Sent` (`BotManager.cs:485, 501, 512`), so Lua is told
  a cast happened when nothing was sent (open since the August audit).

### 2.4 Goal choice and long-horizon stalls

- 35 active quests and 0 available on NewJoker; quest-log-full (40/40) froze all accepts for ClericZero;
  remote accept is refused (2896) and worked around; "The Prelude War" never warps (workaround active).
  Each is a separate crutch in the Lua; none is visible as a goal state with a reason.
- The leveller decides everything every 500 ms from scratch ("the full pass decides afresh",
  `level_quest.lua` `tick`), which is why commitment has to be re-added by hand per phase (`fightCont`,
  `fullPending`, sticky children).

## 3. Architecture

### 3.1 God classes (C#)

| file | lines | what it mixes |
|---|---|---|
| `Session/ZoneView.cs` | 3,868 | packet decoding for ~60 opcodes, world state, combat statistics, aggro learning, learned scalars, heal model, trade state, quest state, logging |
| `Manager/BotManager.cs` | 3,855 (108 public methods) | bot lifecycle, login/handoff loop, every action's protocol sequence, travel planner, gate discovery, watchdogs, mount handling |
| `Scripting/BotApi.cs` | 2,498 (325 public members) | the Lua facade, navigation (`RouteAndWalk` is here), kiting geometry, quest planning helpers, marshalling |
| `Host/BotEndpoints.cs` | 2,091 | ~120 HTTP routes, DTO shaping, quest board logic (including baked mob-id ranges) |
| `Host/Pages/watch.html` | 1,743 | the whole UI in one file |

Consequences: protocol sequences live in the manager but their result tracking lives in `ZoneView`;
navigation lives in the Lua facade; the HTTP layer contains game logic. There are 981 lines of tests
across 8 files for ~24,000 lines of C#.

### 3.2 The Lua leveller

`scripts/level_quest.lua` (private repo): 10,475 lines, 307 functions, **707 top-level locals**,
40 distinct phases set from 50 call sites, 239 distinct `bot.*` functions at 2,170 call sites, 281
`crutch(...)` calls, 222 `return true` early exits, 461 one-line guard returns. The largest regions
between top-level function definitions are ~2,150 lines (around `healStep`), ~570 (`aonce`), ~540
(`rangedPuller`), ~360 (`kiteStep`), ~310 (`castRotation`).

- **No structure for decisions.** `tick()` is a 47-line dispatcher into a fast/full split; the full pass
  is a priority cascade where each branch returns early. The order of `if` blocks *is* the policy, which
  is how the August "stale map" wedge happened (the refresh sat behind the branch the stale value kept
  taking).
- **Upvalue and forward-reference hazards** are common enough to have their own lint test
  (`ScriptForwardRefTests`) and an open ticket for the case it misses.
- **Caching is ad hoc** (section 1.3).
- **Cross-cutting logic duplicated with C#**: mount item class 23 (`BotManager.cs:623` and the Lua),
  attack ranges, NPC selection.
- **The existing graph runner is unused.** `BehaviorGraphRunner` + `GraphStore` + `/api/graphs` implement
  states and transitions (Layer 2/3 of the project plan) but the leveller does not use them; the only
  graphs are the two-state June prototype in `scripts/graph/`.

### 3.3 Coupling and contracts

- `BotApi` returns `bool` "sent" for actions; there is no handle to the outcome. Results arrive as
  ad-hoc events or as state that a later tick must interpret (e.g. the tent `ackAgoMs < now - sentAt`
  dance in `tentTick`).
- There is no versioned contract between the C# API and the Lua: the Lua probes with `botHas()` and
  `pcall` around members that may not exist (the August audit counted 37 `bot.X and bot.X()` guards that
  throw instead of guarding).
- The host process mixes the bot runtime, the web UI, metrics, knowledge persistence and account
  provisioning; a rebuild stops every bot and wipes ring buffers.

## 5. Correctness: assumptions and hacks vs the game's rules

Each item: what the bot assumes (file:line), the game's rule, and the acceptance test that confirms it.

### 5.1 Auto-attack (bash) reach — S1
- **Bot:** a single learned scalar `LearnedMeleeRange` from the second-highest distance a swing landed
  at, seeded from persisted knowledge and **capped at 150 u** (`ZoneView.cs:885-895`, `:1093`, `:1277`).
  The Lua adds `ATTACK_RANGE_MELEE = 100`, archer 450, and approaches to `MELEE = 45`
  (`level_quest.lua:13`, `:31`).
- **Rule:** a swing is kept while `distance <= (targetBody + weaponReach) * 1.25`, with
  `weaponReach = 120` u for every weapon except bows and crossbows (`600` u), and
  `targetBody = MobInfo.AbsoluteSize * 50 / 1000` (client `MobInfo.shn`; a player's body is `50`). Against
  a Slime (`AbsoluteSize 200`, body 10) that is 162.5 u, already above the learner's cap; against a large
  mob it is far more. Distances are measured between the server's positions.
- **Test:** stand at 155 u from a Slime and at 160 u from a large mob, bash, read the SWING_DAMAGE stream.

### 5.2 Skill cast range — S1
- **Bot:** `NeedsFacingAdjust` compares the raw `ActiveSkill.Range` (or the learned melee scalar for
  range 0) with the centre distance (`BotManager.cs:702-745`, `:722`); the Lua keeps its own margins.
- **Rule:** `reach = ActiveSkill.Range + max(50, Range * 10%) + targetBody`, then the accepted distance
  is `min(reach * 1.25, reach + 60)`. Outside it the cast is refused with `0x0FCA`.
- **Test:** cast at `reach * 1.24` and `reach + 61` against mobs of two sizes; expect accept / `0x0FCA`.

### 5.3 Line of fire and the attack-block layer — S1
- **Bot:** loads only `<map>.shbd` (`BlockGrid.Load`, `BlockGrid.cs:53`).
- **Rule:** maps that ship a `<map>.shab` (attack block) treat its blocked cells as blocked for movement
  too (the two layers are OR-ed) and refuse a cast or swing when the attack-block layer cuts the line to
  the target (cast refused `0x0FD1`, bash silently stops). Maps with a `.shab` in `9Data/Shine/BlockInfo`:
  `RouTemDn01` (Luminous Stone 1, where NewMage logged 6 deaths and 23 OUTMATCHED in 10 minutes on
  2026-10-08), `Tower02`, `UrgDragon`, `Leviathan`, `KDArena`, `KDCake`, `KDHBat1`, `KDWater`. The
  correlation with RouTemDn01 is unproven; the missing layer is not.
- **Test:** on RouTemDn01, count `0x0FD1` refusals and swings that stop without a BASHSTOP reason
  before/after loading the layer.

### 5.4 Movement acceptance — S1
- **Bot:** treats every MOVEFAIL as a wall it walked into and may record a learned runtime block at the
  snap-back point (`BotManager.cs` ~3289, `BlockGrid.RtBlocked`); validates its own routes with an exact
  supercover test plus a clearance band.
- **Rule** (the MOVEFAIL carries the server's position):
  1. the `from` of a move must be within **200 u** of the server's current position, otherwise the move
     is refused - a desync over 200 u makes *every* move fail on open ground;
  2. the destination is accepted if **any** tile of the 3x3 block around it is walkable;
  3. the straight segment is checked from the **server's** current position, sampled one unit at a time
     along the major axis; it is refused only when **two consecutive samples** are blocked **and** all
     four points 10 u either side of the sample are blocked. Thin walls and corners shorter than that are
     passable; the bot's exact clip test is stricter than the game.
  4. a STOP is validated the same way (position within 200 u, 3x3 rule).
- **Consequences for the bot:** many "MOVEFAIL on OPEN GROUND" and "wedged off-grid" cases in the
  kanban are rule 1 (desync), for which a learned wall is the wrong lesson; the shared learned-block set
  then poisons other bots (section 1.4).
- **Test:** send a MOVERUN with `from` 199 u and 201 u off the last server position; send a destination
  on a blocked tile with one free neighbour; walk a segment that grazes a 1-tile corner.

### 5.5 Moving cancels attacks and casts — S1
- **Bot:** the kite loop and the "face-step" logic move freely around casts; the Lua guards some cases
  ("NEVER CAST WITH A WALK IN FLIGHT") but the walk task streams MOVERUN steps on its own
  (`BotManager.WalkPath` pacing loop, `:2803-2823`).
- **Rule:** any MOVERUN cancels the casting skill and stops auto-attack (except skills flagged for casting
  while moving); after a move the bot must re-send BASHSTART; BASHSTART is refused while the previous
  swing's interval has not elapsed. A mounted character cannot cast (`0x0FC0`).
- **Test:** cast a skill with cast time, send one MOVERUN mid-bar, read the cast result; bash, move one
  step, read whether swings continue.

### 5.6 Mob chase leash — S1
- **Bot:** learns a per-mob-id "chase limit" as the distance from the mob's *spawn anchor* when it starts
  chasing (`ZoneView.cs:743-780`, `FreezeMobAnchor`, `MobChaseLimit`).
- **Rule:** a chasing mob gives up when it is further than its chase distance (`FollowCha`) from **the
  point where it was last damaged**; every hit re-anchors it (except mobs with a reset interval). A mob
  further than 3,000 u from its spawn stops acquiring new targets. So a bot that keeps hitting while
  retreating never sheds it; one that stops hitting sheds it after `FollowCha` of running from the last
  hit point (typically 1,500 u).
- **Test:** pull a mob, stop hitting, run straight; measure the distance from the last-hit position when
  it turns back; repeat while hitting every 3 s.

### 5.7 Aggro detection — S3 (already right)
- The bot already models the detect shape correctly (circle of radius `DetectCha` centred 0.4 r ahead of
  the mob's facing, >10 level gap ignored except the "no level" type; `ZoneView.cs:658-720`). Keep it.
  The learning is per mob id with a monotone max; it should become an estimator with a confidence
  (section 6).

### 5.8 Family assist ("adds") — S2
- **Bot:** no model; adds are discovered when they hit.
- **Rule:** damaging a mob calls nearby mobs of the same family within its family radius to help.
  This is what turns a single pull into a pack on Marlone and Luminous Stone fields.
- **Test:** hit an edge mob of a family group, record which others aggro and their distances; learn the
  radius per family.

### 5.9 Mob reach and timing — S2
- **Bot:** learns per-mob max/avg hit (`LearnedMobHits`); no reach or interval model.
- **Rule:** a mob hits when within `MobWeapon.Range + targetBody`, swings every ~`AtkSpd + AtkDly`, picks
  skill rows by the per-row rate down the list. These are server-only numbers; the bot must learn them
  (section 6), but the *shape* is known, so it can estimate two parameters instead of guessing a policy.

## 6. Data derivation (server-only facts)

The bot is a client: at runtime it may use the client SHN files and what the wire tells it. Server-only
tables (`MobInfoServer`, `MobWeapon`, `MobAttackSequence`) stay out of the runtime, as today. What can
improve is **how** the bot learns them.

| quantity | today | better |
|---|---|---|
| mob detect radius `DetectCha` | per-mob monotone max of a bound (`ZoneView.CommitDetect`) | an interval estimate per mob id (lower bound from "noticed us at d", upper bound from "did not notice us at d while facing us and in sight"), snapped to the discrete set the game uses: across all 2,878 mobs there are only 43 distinct values for aggressive mobs (offline check against `MobInfoServer`); share across bots |
| chase distance `FollowCha` | distance from the spawn anchor (wrong origin, 5.6) | distance from the last-hit point when the mob turns back; typical value 1,500 u gives a strong prior |
| melee/skill reach | learned scalar | exact formula from client data (5.1, 5.2); nothing to learn |
| mob reach `MobWeapon.Range` | not modelled | one parameter per mob: the largest distance a mob swing landed at minus our body (50), snapped to observed values |
| mob attack interval | not modelled | median swing-to-swing interval per mob id from SWING_DAMAGE timestamps |
| mob damage | per-mob max/avg hit | the damage formula is already read (`docs/DAMAGE_FORMULA.md`); fit the mob's two unknowns (`WC` range and `AC`) by least squares over hits taken and dealt, with our own stats known exactly |
| mob armour (`AC`, `MR`) | not modelled | fit from our hits: damage dealt given our known attack power |
| family assist radius | not modelled | per family (`MobInfo` InxName family), max distance of an add that aggroed within 1 s of our first hit |
| HP/SP regen out of combat | "standing regen absent" flag after 20 s | measure per state (standing, sitting, tent) once and persist |
| soul stone cooldown / heal | learned min success gap / avg heal | the item use effect is in client data (`ItemInfo` + the use-effect table); verify then remove the learner |
| dungeon classification | median-HP ratio vs field mobs (1.5x) | keep; add measured damage taken per mob as the second signal (open ticket) |

Validation without runtime use: an offline test compares every learned estimate in the shared knowledge
store with `MobInfoServer`/`MobWeapon` and reports bias and coverage. This is how "fit curves and ranges"
becomes measurable.
